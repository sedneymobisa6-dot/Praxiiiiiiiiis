Registration

Not everyone must
register. But most
already qualify.

If your organisation collects, stores, or uses personal data, the law already applies to you. Whether you must register depends on two things — your size, and what you do.

2
Conditions for exemption
Annual turnover below KES 5M and fewer than 10 employees. Both must be true.
The exception that catches most businesses
Even if you are small, you must still register if you process data for any listed purpose — education, health, finance, hospitality, property, telecom, transport, direct marketing, gambling, CCTV, or genetic data.

03 / 05
Process

Six steps.
No guesswork.

1
Determine your role
Are you a data controller, a data processor, or both? Your obligations depend on this.
2
Confirm whether you are exempt
Check the size threshold — then check the purpose list. If you fall under any listed purpose, you are not exempt.
3
Prepare your documents
Establishment documents, purpose of processing, categories of data subjects, description of data, and security measures.
4
Apply through the ODPC portal
Complete Form DPR1 online. Attach your documents and particulars.
5
Pay the prescribed fee
Fees vary by category — micro, medium, large, public entity, charity.
6
Receive your certificate
Issued within 14 days if approved. Valid for 24 months. Then you renew.

Roles

Three parties.
One law.
Different duties.

Controller
The decision-maker.
Determines why and how personal data is processed. Carries primary legal responsibility. Answerable to the ODPC and to data subjects.
Processor
The executor.
Processes data on behalf of the controller. No decision-making power. Liable only if it acts outside instructions, or violates processor-specific obligations.
Subject
The person at the centre.
The identifiable natural person whose data is processed. Holds the rights — access, rectification, erasure, objection, portability. The law exists to protect them.
